# Research: EU VAT on digital services for a small SaaS

## Question
Reader: a founder of a small SaaS, often outside the EU (US, UK, India), sometimes in one EU country, who sells monthly subscriptions online to people and businesses in the EU. As of November 2026 they want to know: do I owe EU VAT from the first sale, what is the €10,000 threshold and does it apply to me, what does OSS involve in practice (registration, returns, records), what changes when my customers are businesses, and when is a merchant of record simpler or cheaper than doing it myself on Stripe.

## Sources
- https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/register-oss_en (European Commission, OSS portal, "Register"; read 2026-11-21): the non-Union scheme is for "any taxable person, not established in the EU, who supplies services to non-taxable persons"; such a business picks any member state as its member state of identification. No date shown.
- https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/declare-and-pay-oss_en (European Commission, OSS portal, "Declare and pay"; read 2026-11-21): quarterly returns for the non-Union and Union schemes; due by the end of the month after the quarter (30 Apr, 31 Jul, 31 Oct, 31 Jan), not moved for weekends or holidays; can't be filed before the quarter ends; one currency, generally euro, ECB rate on the last day of the period; a nil return is required for a quarter with no EU sales; corrections in a later return within three years; reminders on day 10, then penalties under each consumption country's rules; three consecutive reminders unresolved can lead to exclusion.
- https://vat-one-stop-shop.ec.europa.eu/one-stop-shop/record-keeping-and-audits-oss_en (European Commission, OSS portal, record keeping; read 2026-11-21): records kept 10 years from the end of the year of the transaction, even after leaving the scheme; must include the consumption country, the type and date of supply, VAT payable, and the information used to place the customer; available electronically to any consumption country on request; not providing them within a month of a reminder counts as persistent non-compliance.
- https://www.revenue.ie/en/vat/vat-on-services/electronic-services/vat-and-electronically-supplied-services/index.aspx (Irish Revenue, published 6 Jul 2026; read 2026-11-21): the €10,000 threshold (TBE services plus intra-Community distance sales, current and preceding calendar year) applies only to a supplier established in one member state; "does not apply" to a supplier not established in the EU; electronically supplied services include software and upgrades, services that provide a business presence on a network, automated internet services.
- https://www.revenue.ie/en/vat/foreign-suppliers-doing-business-in-ireland/non-established-traders-supplying-goods.aspx (Irish Revenue, published 10 Dec 2025; read 2026-11-21): B2B services are generally supplied where the recipient is established, and the recipient self-accounts on the reverse charge basis; exception: non-EU suppliers of electronic services (hosting, software, online data) to private individuals resident in Ireland must register.
- https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/taxation/vat/cross-border-vat/index_en.htm (Your Europe, European Commission, last checked 13 Jul 2026; read 2026-11-21): TBE services always taxed in the customer's country (with the €10,000 exception for small EU sellers); B2B: the customer pays VAT at its own rate under the reverse charge.
- https://europa.eu/youreurope/business/taxation/vat/vat-rules-rates/index_en.htm (Your Europe, last checked 13 Jul 2026; read 2026-11-21): standard rates for all 27 countries, LU 17% lowest, HU 27% highest; used in `vat-math.py`.
- https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_impl/2017/2459/oj/eng (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2017/2459; EUR-Lex refused our fetcher, text read in the LawPlayer mirror https://lawplayer.com/eu/act/32017R2459 on 2026-11-21): new Art. 24b of Regulation 282/2011, from 1 Jan 2019: the supplier places a consumer on two items of non-contradictory evidence from Art. 24f; a supplier with an establishment in a member state whose TBE supplies to other member states stay at or under €100,000 (current and preceding year) may rely on one item from a third party (e.g. the payment provider). A non-EU supplier gets no such relief.
- https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/european-union (Stripe docs, read 2026-11-21): non-EU businesses "might need to register to collect VAT from your first sale"; non-Union OSS needs no tax representative; the 10,000 EUR "small seller option" is for EU businesses; reverse charge when the customer gives a European VAT number; Stripe Tax prioritizes a single address as the customer's location for EU digital services but stores all location evidence; Stripe files non-Union OSS returns in Ireland for eligible businesses.
- https://stripe.com/tax/pricing (Stripe, read 2026-11-21, served in CA$): Tax Basic 0.5% per transaction where registered (no-code); Tax Complete from CA$120 a month on a one-year contract, entry tier 2 registrations, 200 transactions a month, 4 filings a year; Stripe registers and files directly for the EU non-Union OSS. - https://www.paddle.com/help/sell/tax/how-paddle-handles-vat-on-your-behalf (Paddle help, no date; read 2026-11-21): Paddle is the seller to the buyer, registered in 100+ jurisdictions, remits the tax; a cross-border business buyer with a valid VAT ID pays no VAT to Paddle and self-accounts; the sale from the developer to Paddle is B2B, usually reverse charge.
- https://taxation-customs.ec.europa.eu/taxation/vat/vat-digital-age-vida_en (European Commission, ViDA; read 2026-11-21): adopted 11 Mar 2025; from 1 Jul 2028: extension of the OSS schemes and mandatory reverse charge for non-identified suppliers (Single VAT Registration).
- https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/vat-notice-7001-should-i-be-registered-for-vat/vat-notice-7001-should-i-be-registered-for-vat (HMRC VAT Notice 700/1, updated 5 Aug 2026; read 2026-11-21): a non-established taxable person making taxable supplies in the UK must register "regardless of their value and including supplies of digital services".

## Our own data (2026-11-21, scripts and raw output in this folder)
- **`vat-math.py`** (rates from Your Europe, population from Eurostat): simple mean standard rate 21.91%, population-weighted 21.27% (EU27 451,990,314 people on 1 Jan 2026). A VAT-inclusive €29 price keeps €24.79 in Luxembourg and €22.83 in Hungary, €1.95 a month apart per customer. Example A (100 consumers spread like the population, $29 a month, VAT on top): $34,800 net sales a year, about $7,400 of VAT through OSS, about $1,850 per quarterly return (converted to euro at the ECB rate on the quarter's last day).
- **MoR premium, from our October 2026 fee tables** (topic 5, card issued outside the US, the normal case for EU buyers): Paddle minus Stripe + Billing + Tax Basic is +15¢ at $9, +3¢ at $29 and −39¢ at $99 per sale; at $29 × 1,200 sales a year that's $31. For B2B only (no EU registration, so no Stripe Tax fee), the premium is +17¢ per $29 sale, $205 a year. Stripe Tax Complete adds $1,080 a year at $90 a month.
- **VIES availability** (`vies-status-2026-11-21.tsv`, the `check-status` endpoint polled every 5 minutes by `poll-vies.sh`): at the first poll Belgium and Finland reported "Unavailable". Results summarised in the post from the file.

## Disagreements
- **"VAT from the first sale" vs "a €10,000 threshold".** Many guides quote the €10,000 threshold without saying who it's for. Irish Revenue and Stripe both say it is only for a seller established in one EU country; a non-EU seller owes VAT from the first consumer sale. We follow Revenue.
- **Location evidence.** The regulation says two non-contradictory items (one third-party item under €100,000 only for EU-established sellers). Stripe's docs say Stripe Tax uses a single address for the calculation but stores all evidence. Not a contradiction: what you charge vs what you can prove. The post tells non-EU sellers to keep two items per customer.
- **Is a merchant of record "expensive"?** Most comparisons quote 5% + 50¢ against Stripe's 2.9% + 30¢. With the international-card surcharge, Billing and Tax Basic added, the gap on a $29 sale from an EU card is 3¢ (our calculation), and it reverses at $99. For B2B-only sellers, who have no OSS filings to save, the premium is real.
- **Reverse charge for non-EU sellers.** The Your Europe page explains it for EU sellers; Revenue's page confirms the recipient self-accounts for B2B services from abroad. Stripe notes some countries add conditions it doesn't model. The post keeps to the general rule and says to check a country's conditions.
- **Stripe Tax Complete price.** The pricing page shows local currency (CA$120 to us); the US page read in October said $90. The post quotes the US figure with its date and notes the entry tier's limits.
